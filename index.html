Research approach

How I work

Four principles that shape every study, from scoping to stakeholder readout.

I work backwards from the decision

The brief rarely names the real risk. I find out what would actually change product direction before designing a study. If I cannot name the decision a study will inform, I do not run it.

Findings are framed as tradeoffs

I do not hand over observations. I hand over a choice, with the cost of each option made explicit. A readout that does not change what the team does next did not land.

Method choice is a hypothesis

I pick methods because they answer a specific question, not because they fit the timeline. Qual reveals mechanism. Quant tests whether it holds. I sequence them so each phase narrows what the next needs to find out.

Research that scales past the study

A study that answers one question once is the floor, not the goal. I build reusable assets from every project: decision frameworks, screeners, synthesis templates. The Conversational vs. UI framework from the Joule study is now used independently by the team on new AI feature proposals.

Background

I think about where products break before they ship.

My background is in Human Factors and Cognitive Science, fields built on the premise that systems fail in predictable ways when designed without accounting for how people actually behave. Before any study begins, I ask: what are we assuming about our users, and have we validated it?

I work closely with designers, PMs, and engineers throughout the process, not just at the readout. I write deliverables that help stakeholders make faster, better-calibrated decisions.

Methods: In-depth interviews · usability testing · contextual inquiry · workshop facilitation · A/B testing · surveys · pre/post benchmarking · eye tracking · FDA usability (IEC 62366)
