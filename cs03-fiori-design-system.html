03 · SAP Fiori Design System · Aug–Sep 2025

Designers were abandoning a working design system. The problem wasn't quality. It was trust.

Satisfaction with SAP Fiori Mobile dropped to 3.71/5 and teams were quietly building custom components instead of using the ones available. The team's hypothesis pointed to component quality. I challenged that hypothesis, uncovered three structural trust failures, and drove changes that lifted satisfaction by +12.6% within a single quarter.

Problem
Teams were bypassing functional components. A quality fix would have addressed the symptom and missed the underlying cause entirely.
My Role
Lead researcher. Challenged the initial hypothesis, designed and ran the mixed-methods study, facilitated a cross-functional workshop, and drove three changes shipped in v25.04.
Methods
n=19 interviews · live workflow observations · pre/post benchmark survey · cross-functional prioritization workshop
Outcome
+12.6% satisfaction (3.71 → 4.18) in one quarter. ~8 weeks of engineering rework prevented. Three structural changes shipped in v25.04. 2026 roadmap shaped.

Context & Hypothesis Challenge

600+ internal designers at SAP depended on Fiori Mobile. When satisfaction dropped and teams began building custom components, the first instinct was to audit component quality. The behavioral signal told a different story. Teams were bypassing components that worked correctly. Avoiding something functional pointed to a structural cause, not a technical one.

The Reframe
Team's hypothesis
"The components have quality issues. Improve them and adoption returns."
→
My research question
"Why do designers feel unsafe depending on this system, and which dimensions of trust are breaking down?"
Key Findings
1
Conflicting design authorities doubled decision time
SAP guidelines contradicted Apple HIG and Material Design. Every component decision required external cross-referencing. Designers weren't avoiding low-quality components. They were avoiding a system that couldn't tell them what "correct" meant.
2
Rigid components created a debt spiral teams couldn't exit
Any customization required detaching from the component → detaching meant losing future updates → teams built custom alternatives → debt compounded with every release. The system's inflexibility was generating the abandonment it suffered from.
3
Accessibility anxiety produced worse accessibility outcomes
Developers couldn't verify WCAG compliance in the system, so they built custom components they believed were safe. Those builds were objectively less accessible. Unverifiable risk produced worse outcomes than the risk itself.

"Sometimes I spend more time figuring out which component to use than actually designing."

— Internal designer, live workflow observation
Driving Consensus Across a Skeptical Team
The challenge
The team had already committed to a quality hypothesis. Shifting the frame to investigate trust required presenting the behavioral evidence. Teams bypassing working components was a signal strong enough to warrant a different investigation entirely.
  • Facilitated a cross-functional prioritization workshop. Buy-in was built through co-prioritization, not presentation
  • Interviewed designers and developers separately to surface role-specific trust failures before bringing groups together
  • Framed each root cause as structural rather than attributable to any individual, reducing defensiveness and enabling action
Changes Shipped in v25.04
  • Comparison tables documenting where SAP intentionally diverges from Apple and Android guidelines, with the rationale made explicit
  • Flexible component variants with clearly defined customization zones, enabling adaptation without forcing detachment
  • Screen reader annotations integrated into every component spec, removing accessibility ambiguity at the point of decision
Impact
+12.6% satisfaction in one quarter (3.71 → 4.18) ~8 weeks of engineering rework prevented 3 structural changes shipped in v25.04 2026 roadmap shaped by trust framework

On ~8 weeks: PM estimate based on average sprint cost of custom component detachment cycles observed during research. Each detachment forced a rebuild that the PM team calculated at ~2 sprint weeks of engineering time. Three identified structural blockers contributed to at least 4 such cycles in the 6 months prior to the study. Conservative estimate; actual value was likely higher given compounding downstream effects on QA and accessibility review.

Reflection
Use a diary study to capture abandonment decisions as they happen
Workflow observations captured a snapshot of behavior. A short diary study (1–2 weeks) would have revealed when teams made the detachment decision in real time rather than reconstructed after the fact, strengthening the causal chain and making the root cause harder to dispute.
